# LAND OF FIRE MISSION TRAVEL & ENCOUNTER ENGINE

**Third Age — Year -15**
*DM Reference Document / AI Game Master Reference*

---

## 1. PURPOSE

This document is the authoritative travel-event framework for missions conducted through the Land of Fire during the later stretch of the war in Year -15.

The table is designed for a Game Master or AI Game Master to generate dynamic travel events while a shinobi squad is moving through Fire Country.

The system emphasizes:

- Combat encounters
- Bingo Book targets and investigations
- Secret, lost, forbidden, experimental, and advanced techniques
- Enemy operations and shinobi activity
- Land of Fire geography and wilderness
- Villages, civilians, roads, and wartime activity
- Mission complications
- Third Age political tensions
- Rare A-Rank and S-Rank threats
- Multi-roll encounter chains
- Recurring enemies and evolving encounters

This is an **encounter engine**, not merely a list of isolated random encounters. Results should be connected whenever doing so creates a stronger and more logical story.

---

## 2. CAMPAIGN CONTEXT

### ERA

**Third Age — Year -15.** The campaign takes place during the later stretch of a major war.

The Land of Fire remains strategically important, but the prolonged conflict has created:

- Increased shinobi patrols
- Enemy scouts and infiltrators
- Missing-nin and deserters
- Damaged roads and bridges
- Refugees and displaced civilians
- Smuggling routes
- Hidden enemy bases
- Political distrust
- Secret jutsu research
- Experimental techniques
- Stolen village secrets
- Increased Bingo Book activity
- Battles away from official fronts
- Assassinations and espionage
- Rival shinobi operations

The Land of Fire should feel like a living war zone even when the squad is not directly involved in a major battle.

## 3. CORE DM INSTRUCTIONS

### 3.1 WHEN TO ROLL

Use the travel table when:

- The squad begins a significant travel segment.
- The squad enters a new geographic region.
- The squad spends a prolonged period traveling.
- The squad establishes camp.
- The squad reaches a village, road, forest, river, mountain, or other meaningful location.
- The journey begins to feel uneventful.
- The DM needs an organic complication.
- A mission needs a side encounter.
- The AI GM needs to introduce a clue, enemy, technique, or political development.

**Do not roll constantly.** A single meaningful travel segment may use one roll. A long mission may use several.

---

## 4. STANDARD TRAVEL PROCEDURE

When generating a travel encounter:

### STEP 1 — Determine Location

Identify the squad's current:

- Region
- Terrain
- Road
- Village
- Forest
- River
- Mountain
- Battlefield
- Strategic area

### STEP 2 — Determine Mission

Review:

- Current mission
- Objective
- Destination
- Known enemies
- Known allies
- Time constraints
- Mission rank

### STEP 3 — Determine Current Conditions

Consider:

- Squad health
- Available resources
- Weather
- Time of day
- Stealth status
- Whether the squad is being followed
- Whether the squad is pursuing someone
- Whether the squad is carrying important information or equipment

### STEP 4 — Roll

Roll **1d100** for the core table.
For additional variety, the DM may roll **1d150** using the expanded table.

### STEP 5 — Interpret

Do not treat the result as completely isolated. Check whether:

- A related encounter already occurred
- A Bingo Book target is already involved
- A secret technique has already been discovered
- A faction is already active
- An enemy has already appeared
- The mission has already been compromised

### STEP 6 — Escalate if Appropriate

Apply the Escalation Rule (Section 6).

### STEP 7 — Resolve

Determine whether the result becomes:

- Combat
- Investigation
- Exploration
- Social encounter
- Pursuit
- Rescue
- Discovery
- Mission complication
- Political event
- Major threat

### STEP 8 — Record Important Events

If an encounter creates a recurring NPC, enemy, technique, faction, clue, or consequence, record it as campaign history (Section 32).

## 5. MODULAR ENCOUNTER RULE

Every result can be combined with another result. Do not assume two rolls are unrelated.

**Example**

> `03` — Abandoned Shinobi Camp
> followed later by:
> `125` — Bingo Book Target's Signature Technique
>
> *Possible interpretation:* The abandoned camp belonged to the Bingo Book target. The squad discovers evidence showing that the target has developed or possesses a unique technique.

**Another example**

> `17` — Enemy Scouts
> followed by:
> `145` — False Flag Operation
>
> *Possible interpretation:* The scouts are preparing an operation designed to make one shinobi village believe another village attacked Fire Country.

The DM should prefer connected encounters when the connection is logical and improves the story.

---

## 6. ESCALATION RULE

### OFFICIAL RULE

If the same encounter category is rolled twice during the same mission or travel sequence, the second result should escalate, explain, or connect to the first whenever reasonably possible.

If the same category occurs three or more times, the DM should consider turning the events into:

- A developing subplot
- A recurring enemy
- A Bingo Book hunt
- A secret-technique investigation
- A political conspiracy
- A mission complication
- A larger enemy operation
- A major story event

**Example**

> `36` — Low-Priority Bingo Book Target
> followed by:
> `44` — Target's Hideout
> The hideout belongs to the exact target encountered previously.
> Then:
> `125` — Target's Signature Technique
> The squad learns something important about that same target.

The encounter has now naturally become a developing Bingo Book Hunt.

## 11. BINGO BOOK INTEGRATION

### CORE RULE

The travel table determines **HOW** the squad encounters a Bingo Book target.
The Bingo Book determines **WHO** the target is.

When a result says `[BINGO BOOK]`:

1. Identify the appropriate threat level.
2. Reference the current Bingo Book.
3. Select an existing target whenever possible.
4. Use that target's established identity.
5. Use that target's established rank.
6. Use their established abilities.
7. Use their established techniques.
8. Use their established personality.
9. Use their established affiliations.
10. Use their established bounty.
11. Use their established known information.
12. Determine the target's current objective.
13. Determine how that objective intersects with the squad.

**Do not create a generic enemy when an established Bingo Book target is appropriate.**

---

## 12. BINGO BOOK TARGETS ARE NOT AUTOMATICALLY HOSTILE

A Bingo Book target may:

- Attack
- Flee
- Negotiate
- Offer information
- Attempt recruitment
- Be wounded
- Be hunting someone else
- Be hunted by another group
- Be disguised
- Be protecting an ally
- Be pursuing a secret technique
- Be unaware of the squad
- Attempt to manipulate the squad
- Attempt to use the squad against another enemy

The target's personality and established lore should determine behavior.

- Legendary missing-nin

## 14. HIGH-LEVEL ENCOUNTER SAFETY RULE

A low-level squad may still encounter a high-level Bingo Book target. However, an S-Rank target does not automatically mean an S-Rank battle.

For an inappropriate power mismatch, use:

- Sighting
- Warning
- Tracking
- Evidence
- Pursuit
- Escape
- Rescue
- Survival
- Indirect encounter
- Witnessing the target fight someone else
- Discovering the target's aftermath

**Do not force an unwinnable boss fight simply because a high-level result was rolled.**

## 15. BINGO BOOK HUNT ESCALATION

A Bingo Book storyline may progress through:

- **STAGE 1 — RUMOR:** Someone mentions the target.
- **STAGE 2 — EVIDENCE:** The squad finds tracks, victims, equipment, chakra signatures, messages, safehouses, or witnesses.
- **STAGE 3 — ASSOCIATE:** The squad encounters an informant, apprentice, ally, victim, or accomplice.
- **STAGE 4 — TERRITORY:** The squad enters the target's operating area.
- **STAGE 5 — TECHNIQUE:** The squad witnesses the target's unique ability.
- **STAGE 6 — HUNT:** The squad actively tracks the target.
- **STAGE 7 — FIRST CONTACT:** The target is encountered.
- **STAGE 8 — PURSUIT:** The target escapes or the squad must chase them.
- **STAGE 9 — REMATCH:** The target returns with information about the squad and changes tactics.
- **STAGE 10 — RESOLUTION:** Capture, defeat, escape, negotiation, recruitment, alliance, betrayal, death, or continued rivalry.

The DM may skip stages when appropriate.

## 16. SECRET TECHNIQUE DISCOVERY SYSTEM

A secret technique result is generally a **progression opportunity**, not an automatic reward.

The squad may discover:

- Scroll
- Training notes
- Enemy demonstration
- Battlefield remnants
- Hidden laboratory
- Clan records
- Ancient markings
- Sealing formula
- Summoning contract
- Teacher
- Bingo Book target
- Forbidden research
- Failed experiment

---

## 17. SECRET TECHNIQUE PROGRESSION

- **STAGE 1 — DISCOVERY:** The character discovers evidence that the technique exists.
- **STAGE 2 — INVESTIGATION:** The character determines what the technique requires.
- **STAGE 3 — UNDERSTANDING:** The character learns the principles, chakra manipulation, or prerequisites.
- **STAGE 4 — TRAINING:** The character attempts to reproduce the technique.
- **STAGE 5 — COMPLETION:** The character successfully learns the technique.
- **STAGE 6 — EVOLUTION:** A later discovery, breakthrough, teacher, battle, or training event may unlock an advanced version.

A random encounter should not automatically grant a completed high-level jutsu unless the campaign's existing progression system specifically allows it.

## 20. LAND OF FIRE LOCATION MODIFIERS

The DM should modify encounter interpretation based on the squad's location.

**MAJOR ROADS** — increase likelihood of: merchants, patrols, travelers, enemy scouts, political messengers, ambushes, Bingo Book sightings, supply caravans.

**DEEP FOREST** — increase likelihood of: ambushes, missing-nin, secret camps, secret techniques, wildlife, tracking, hidden laboratories, clan ruins.

**RIVERS** — increase likelihood of: crossings, ambushes, floods, fishing villages, supply movement, water-based techniques.

**MOUNTAIN ROUTES** — increase likelihood of: rockslides, narrow battlefields, hidden caves, bandit activity, enemy observation posts, dangerous falls, isolated settlements.

**VILLAGES** — increase likelihood of: political events, rumors, Bingo Book information, merchants, civilian requests, mission intelligence, shinobi patrols, assassinations.

**WARTIME FRONTS / STRATEGIC AREAS** — increase likelihood of: combat, enemy squads, casualties, supply caravans, captured shinobi, Bingo Book targets, political incidents, forbidden techniques, major battles.

## 21. THREE-ROLL JOURNEY

For a long mission, the DM may use three rolls.

- **ROLL 1 — ENVIRONMENT:** What does the squad encounter?
- **ROLL 2 — COMPLICATION:** What changes the situation?
- **ROLL 3 — ESCALATION:** What makes the encounter more important?

**EXAMPLE**

> Roll 1: `07` — Collapsed Bridge
> Roll 2: `63` — Secret Technique Trap
> Roll 3: `125` — Bingo Book Target's Signature Technique
>
> *Possible interpretation:* The bridge was deliberately destroyed to protect an old technique hidden beneath the crossing. The trap is based on the signature technique of a Bingo Book target currently operating nearby.

---

## 22. FOUR-ROLL MAJOR JOURNEY

For especially important missions:

1. Environment
2. Initial Encounter
3. Complication
4. Escalation

**EXAMPLE**

> Environment: Deep forest
> Initial Encounter: Enemy scouts
> Complication: Mission compromised
> Escalation: S-Rank Bingo Book target
>
> *Possible result:* The scouts were not randomly searching the forest. They already knew the squad was coming because the mission was leaked. The leak originated from someone connected to the S-Rank Bingo Book target.

---

## 23. COMBAT ENCOUNTER GUIDELINES

Combat results should not always mean *"Enemies appear. Roll initiative."*

Determine:

- Who initiated the encounter?
- What does the enemy want?
- Does the enemy know the squad's capabilities?
- Is the enemy trying to kill? Capture? Delay? Steal something? Gather information? Escape? Recruit? Protect someone? Test a technique?

Also consider:

- Terrain
- Weather
- Civilians
- Mission objective
- Other factions
- Secret techniques
- Bingo Book connections

Combat should create information and consequences whenever possible.

---

## 24. ENEMY RETREAT RULE

Enemies may retreat when appropriate. If an enemy escapes, do not treat the encounter as meaningless.

Record:

- What the enemy learned
- What the squad learned
- Where the enemy went
- Whether the enemy was injured
- Whether the enemy changes tactics
- Whether the enemy alerts allies
- Whether the enemy becomes a recurring threat

A successful enemy retreat can naturally generate future Bingo Book encounters, rematches, pursuits, investigations, counter-techniques, and mission complications.

---

## 25. RECURRING ENCOUNTER RULE

If the same enemy or faction appears more than once, evolve their behavior.

- **FIRST ENCOUNTER:** The enemy is unfamiliar with the squad.
- **SECOND ENCOUNTER:** The enemy has learned something about the squad.
- **THIRD ENCOUNTER:** The enemy has prepared specifically for the squad.
- **FOURTH ENCOUNTER:** The enemy may become a recurring rival, Bingo Book target, faction leader, major antagonist, political player, or secret technique researcher.

A random encounter can therefore become an important campaign NPC.

---

## 26. POLITICAL EVENT GUIDELINES

The campaign takes place during the later stretch of the war. Political encounters should reflect:

- War exhaustion
- Suspicion between villages
- Espionage
- Border disputes
- Supply shortages
- Assassination attempts
- Propaganda
- False-flag operations
- Diplomatic missions
- Conflicting shinobi orders
- Civilian displacement
- Secret alliances
- Factional agendas

Political encounters should not automatically reveal the entire truth. The squad may initially encounter only a clue.

## 27. AI GAME MASTER PRIORITY RULES

When an AI uses this document, follow these priorities.

**PRIORITY 1 — EXISTING CAMPAIGN CANON.** Never contradict established campaign facts, character sheets, Bingo Book entries, mission orders, previously established events, established NPCs, or established factions.

**PRIORITY 2 — CURRENT MISSION.** Prefer encounters that can interact with the squad's current assignment.

**PRIORITY 3 — EXISTING NPCS AND ENEMIES.** Reuse established NPCs, enemies, factions, and Bingo Book targets before inventing replacements.

**PRIORITY 4 — GEOGRAPHIC LOGIC.** Use the actual current region and terrain.

**PRIORITY 5 — ESCALATION.** Connect repeated categories or related results.

**PRIORITY 6 — COMBAT.** When combat occurs, determine a logical enemy and objective. Do not generate a generic fight without context.

**PRIORITY 7 — BINGO BOOK.** When a Bingo Book result occurs, reference the Bingo Book before inventing a target.

**PRIORITY 8 — SECRET TECHNIQUES.** When a secret technique appears, create a discovery/progression opportunity rather than automatically granting a completed jutsu.

**PRIORITY 9 — CONSEQUENCES.** Important encounters should be allowed to affect later travel, future missions, NPC relationships, enemy behavior, political developments, the war, and character progression.

**PRIORITY 10 — DO NOT FORCE ENCOUNTERS.** If a result would create an impossible or nonsensical situation, adapt or reroll it.

## 28. AI DECISION TREE

When generating a travel encounter:

1. Where is the squad?
2. What is the current mission?
3. What is the squad's current condition?
4. Has a related encounter happened recently?
5. Roll the table.
6. Identify the encounter category.
7. Apply the Escalation Rule.
8. Check the Bingo Book if applicable.
9. Check existing campaign NPCs and factions.
10. Determine enemy objective or discovery purpose.
11. Determine encounter type: combat, investigation, social, exploration, pursuit, rescue, discovery, mission complication, political event, or major threat.
12. Resolve using established campaign mechanics.
13. Record meaningful consequences.

---

## 29. DO NOT INVENT BINGO BOOK INFORMATION

If a result calls for a Bingo Book target, do not fabricate an existing target's:

- Name
- Rank
- Abilities
- Jutsu
- Bounty
- Personality
- Affiliations
- History
- Weaknesses
- Known locations

If that information already exists in the Bingo Book, use it.

If no suitable target exists, the DM may create a new target. New targets must be clearly marked `[NEW BINGO BOOK ENTRY]` so the target can later be added to the campaign's permanent Bingo Book.

## 33. IMPORTANT PHILOSOPHY

This table should make the Land of Fire feel like a living war zone, not a hallway between missions.

- Not every event needs to become a fight.
- Not every fight needs to become a major story.
- Not every Bingo Book target needs to be killed.
- Not every secret technique needs to be learned.

The goal is to create opportunities. A single roll might become:

- A five-minute roadside encounter
- A tactical battle
- A recurring enemy
- A Bingo Book investigation
- A new jutsu progression path
- A political conspiracy
- A future mission
- A major war event

The DM decides how far an event develops based on player choices.
