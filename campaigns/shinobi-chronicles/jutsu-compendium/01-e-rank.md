Academy-level fundamentals. The techniques every student must master before graduating to genin.

## Ninjutsu & General Skills

### Body Replacement Technique — *also **Substitution Technique***
**Japanese:** Kawarimi no Jutsu (変わり身の術)
**Also filed as:** **Substitution Technique** — the name used throughout **Book IV**, whose entire Reaction system is built on this jutsu. *Same technique, both names valid.*
**Type:** Ninjutsu / General Skill
**Rank:** E
**Description:** The user swaps their own body with a nearby object — classically a log — at the instant an attack lands, so the strike hits the decoy while the user relocates to safety. Execution quality varies enormously with skill: a genin telegraphs the swap with a puff of smoke and a visible log, while jōnin perform it so fluidly that opponents waste entire combinations on substitutions without realizing it. Reactive rather than proactive, requiring the user to correctly read incoming danger.

### Clone Technique
**Japanese:** Bunshin no Jutsu (分身の術)
**Type:** Ninjutsu / Clone Technique
**Rank:** E
**Description:** Produces intangible illusory duplicates of the user. The copies have no physical substance — they cannot strike, be struck, or interact with objects, and attacks pass harmlessly through them. Their value is purely deceptive: splitting an enemy's attention, baiting attacks, masking the user's true position, or padding apparent numbers. The clones cast no shadow and disturb no dust, which is how observant opponents identify them.

### Transformation Technique
**Japanese:** Henge no Jutsu (変化の術)
**Type:** Ninjutsu / Transformation
**Rank:** E
**Description:** Alters the user's outward appearance into that of another person, animal, or object. The disguise is a chakra-maintained shell rather than a true physical change, so it requires continuous chakra expenditure and collapses if the user is struck hard enough, loses concentration, or runs out of chakra. Convincing use demands the transformer also mimic the target's voice, mannerisms, and knowledge — poor performance exposes the ruse faster than any flaw in the transformation itself.

### Rope Escape Technique
**Japanese:** Nawanuke no Jutsu (縄抜けの術)
**Type:** Ninjutsu / General Skill
**Rank:** E
**Description:** A fundamental restraint-escape method allowing the user to free themselves from ropes, cords, and similar bindings. The approach adapts to the situation — dislocating or compressing joints, chakra-assisted loosening, or exploiting slack in the knot work. Essential survival training rather than a combat asset, since a captured shinobi with no escape method is a liability to their entire squad.

### Cloak of Invisibility Technique
**Japanese:** Kakuremino no Jutsu (隠れ蓑の術)
**Type:** Ninjutsu / General Skill
**Rank:** E
**Description:** The user drapes themselves in a cloth or sheet and channels chakra into it so that it takes on the coloration and texture of the surroundings, letting them blend into a wall, fence, or patch of ground. This is camouflage, not true invisibility — it fails against movement, angled viewing, and any competent sensory or ocular technique.

## Taijutsu

### One Thousand Years of Death
**Japanese:** Sennen Goroshi (千年殺し)
**Type:** Taijutsu
**Rank:** E
**Description:** The user forms a tiger hand seal with both hands and drives the extended fingers forcefully into a distracted opponent's backside, launching them forward. Despite the ominous name it is fundamentally a prank, meant for humiliation and surprise rather than damage. Its practical worth lies in the opening it creates: a target sent sprawling and shocked is briefly defenseless.

## Transformation Variants

### Sexy Technique
**Japanese:** Oiroke no Jutsu (お色気の術)
**Type:** Transformation Ninjutsu
**Rank:** E
**Description:** An applied variant of the Transformation Technique, converting the user into an idealized nude adult woman wreathed in strategically placed smoke. Its purpose is psychological disruption — shocking, embarrassing, or stunning a target long enough to create an opening or defuse a confrontation. Effectiveness depends entirely on the target's susceptibility, and it is useless against a composed or uninterested opponent.
