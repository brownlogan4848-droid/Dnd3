# QUICK REFERENCE — COMBAT

## The combo
Up to your rank's limit in **slots**, not actions. Pay every cost. **Bonus Action and Reaction still follow.** **[SHEET]**

**Chain Momentum:** actions 1–3 clean · 4th −1 · 5th −2 · 6th −3 · 7th −4.
Every action that **hits** gives **+2 damage** to the rest of the combo, cumulatively.
**Open:** a target who fails a save grants **advantage** for the rest of that combo.

## Interruption (Rule 5) **[SHEET]**
Stunned, prone, silenced, grappled, blinded, a resource at 0, or losing a clash **ends the combo.** Remaining actions are lost.
**Pushing through your own combo:** 30 Special, or a feature. No free save.

## Reactions
**Usable at any moment**, including mid-combo against any single action. Declare after the roll, before damage. **One per round.**

**The timing is free; the means are gated.** Escaping requires a jutsu, clan ability, ninja tool, or feature. With an empty kit the only option is **Brace** (8 Stamina, reduce one action by 1d6+CON) and you take the rest.

**Substitution** — 8 Chakra / 5 Stamina, native Reaction, known from the Academy **[SHEET]**. Works against **any** action of a combo. Not usable against area attacks.

## The Counter Window
When the **first** action of a combo misses, the defender may cast **any jutsu they know** as a Reaction (normal Chakra, **150% Stamina**). Once per combo, no S-rank. The one situation where a normally-Action technique casts reactively without a feature.

## Clash
| Stage | Roll |
|---|---|
| **1 — The Lock** | d20 + jutsu modifier + modifiers |
| **2 — The Break** | d20 again; **Lock winner adds +5** |

- **Win both → TOTAL OVERPOWER.** +50% damage. Defender winning both **ends the attacker's combo**.
- **Split → MUTUAL DETONATION.** Both take half from the other, both pushed 15 ft, combo continues at **−2**.
- **Overcharge:** between stages, spend the technique's base cost again for **+4**, max twice. **Declare simultaneously, then reveal.**
- **Rank gap of 3+ steps: no clash.** The higher technique consumes the lower.

**Modifiers:** +2 per rank step higher · ±3 nature advantage/disadvantage · +1 per mastery tier · +2 favorable terrain · −1 off-balance (in the window).

## Nature cycle
**Fire → Wind → Lightning → Earth → Water → Fire** (each strong against the one it points to)

| Relationship | Effect |
|---|---|
| Strong against | +1 damage die; target has **disadvantage** on the save |
| Weak against | **Half damage**; target has **advantage** |

Two or more ranks higher **overrides the cycle entirely**.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XP** | 0 | 10 | 25 | 50 | 100 |

**Efficiency** (−15% Chakra, ×3) · **Conditioning** (−15% Stamina, ×3) · **Power** (+1 die, ×2) · **Precision** (+1 hit/DC, ×2) · **Acceleration** (−1 combo slot, ×2, T3+) · **Extension** (+50%, ×2) · **Control** (×2) · **Seal-less** (T3+) · **Reactive** (T3+) · **Adaptation**

# HOW TO RUN IT — THE TEN

1. **Fights are short.** 3–4 rounds. Eight-round slugfests are the wrong genre.
2. **Every mission costs something** — a technique exposed, an NPC lost, a principle bent.
3. **Missions are often one rank harder than briefed.** The setting's most reliable plot engine.
4. **Announce jutsu by name.** NPCs shout their techniques. So should you.
5. **Give every fight a clock** — reinforcements, a ritual, poison, a collapsing structure.
6. **Wall and Story encounters need a visible exit,** signalled before the fight. Retreat is a valid win.
7. **Don't let famous characters solve problems.** Minato and Madara are weather, not cavalry.
8. **The mid-combat flashback is legitimate.** It's the genre's signature move.
9. **Let players talk to enemies mid-fight.** Sometimes let it work.
10. **Teachers die and students continue.** Build a generational handoff into every campaign.

**And the eleventh, which matters most:** characters only know what they could actually know. **Book III §4.**
