### PART ONE — DEEP BACKGROUND

Context your NPCs may reference as history, legend, or grievance.

---

#### The Mythic Age

##### The Ōtsutsuki Arrival
Kaguya Ōtsutsuki descended from beyond the world, consumed the fruit of the God Tree, and became the first being to wield chakra. She ended the era of endless human warfare by force, then was overthrown by her sons Hagoromo and Hamura. **Nobody in either war era knows any of this** — see Book III §4.

##### The Sage of Six Paths
Hagoromo Ōtsutsuki, the Sage of Six Paths, spread chakra to humanity as ninshū, a spiritual discipline meant to unite people through mutual understanding. His sons Indra and Asura inherited opposing philosophies — power versus cooperation — and their descendants became the **Uchiha** and **Senju**. Their conflict recurs across generations.

*In-era status:* Known as religious folklore. Treated by most shinobi the way a modern soldier treats scripture — culturally present, not operationally real.

---

#### The Warring States Period
*Centuries before Year 0.*

No villages, only mercenary clans hired by feuding noble houses. The Uchiha and Senju are the two strongest and are deliberately hired against each other by clients who profit from their mutual destruction. Children fight and die routinely; life expectancy is brutal. Hashirama Senju and Madara Uchiha meet as boys, become friends, and are set against each other by their fathers.

---

#### The Founding of the Hidden Villages
*Approx. 90–70 years before Year 0.* **[INF]**

Hashirama Senju and Madara Uchiha broker peace and found **Konohagakure**, the first hidden village. The model spreads: the other four great nations found their own villages, and the daimyō system of state-sponsored shinobi forces replaces free-agent clan warfare.

**Key consequences:**
- The **Kage** title is created; Hashirama becomes First Hokage.
- The tailed beasts, captured by Hashirama, are distributed among the villages at the Kage Summit to establish a balance of power. This is the origin of the jinchūriki system.
- Madara defects, believing the Uchiha are being politically neutered. He and Hashirama fight at the **Valley of the End**. Madara is believed killed.
- The Uchiha are gradually relocated to the edge of Konoha and given control of the Military Police Force — a promotion that is also a containment measure.

---

#### The First Great Ninja War
*Approx. 60–50 years before Year 0.* **[INF]**

The village system's first stress test. Fought over resources and border authority. Details are sparse in canon. **Tobirama Senju**, Second Hokage, dies in this era covering the retreat of his squad, naming Hiruzen Sarutobi his successor. The Land of Whirlpools' **Uzushiogakure** begins its decline.

---

#### The Second Great Ninja War
*Approx. 35–25 years before Year 0.* **[INF]**

- **Uzushiogakure is destroyed** by a coalition that fears Uzumaki sealing mastery. Survivors scatter; Konoha shinobi wear the Uzumaki spiral on their uniforms in memoriam. Kushina Uzumaki is relocated to Konoha.
- **Hanzō of the Salamander** dominates the fighting in the Land of Rain. He defeats Jiraiya, Tsunade, and Orochimaru, spares them, and names them **the Legendary Sannin**.
- **Nawaki**, Tsunade's younger brother, is killed on his birthday shortly after receiving her grandfather's necklace.
- Jiraiya remains in Amegakure afterward and trains three war orphans — **Yahiko, Nagato, and Konan**.

### PART TWO — ERA A: THE THIRD GREAT NINJA WAR

**Approx. Year −20 to Year −14.** **[INF]**

---

#### Causes

Unresolved territorial disputes from the Second War, economic strain across all five nations, and a collapse of the fragile deterrence the tailed-beast distribution was meant to guarantee. Every great nation commits. Crucially, almost none of the fighting happens on great-nation soil — the war is fought across **the Land of Rain, the Land of Grass, and the border countries**, which are devastated.

**The defining bilateral conflict is Konoha vs. Iwagakure.**

---

#### The Fronts

##### The Iwa Front (Konoha vs. Iwagakure)
The heaviest theater. Fought through the Land of Grass and the mountainous Earth border. This is where **Kannabi Bridge** stands — Iwa's supply artery.

**Minato Namikaze** turns the front almost single-handedly. Using the **Flying Thunder God Technique**, he annihilates a force of roughly a thousand Iwa shinobi in a single engagement. Iwagakure issues a **flee-on-sight order** against him — the only such standing order given to an entire village's forces in the setting. He is known thereafter as **Konoha's Yellow Flash** (*Konoha no Kiiroi Senkō*). **[M]**

*Iwa's grudge against Konoha is generational and never really ends. Ōnoki still carries it into the Fourth War.*

##### The Suna Front (Konoha vs. Sunagakure)
Suna fights Konoha, and also skirmishes with Iwa. Sunagakure is economically strangled during this period; the Land of Wind's daimyō begins outsourcing contracts, a decision that eventually gutted the village's budget. Chiyo's poison and puppet corps are central to Suna's war effort.

##### The Kumo Front (Konoha vs. Kumogakure)
Lower-intensity but persistent. Kumo's doctrine of raw power and its aggressive pursuit of bloodlines make it a constant threat. The **Third Raikage** personally holds a position against an army of ten thousand shinobi from a combined force, fighting for three days and nights, and dies of exhaustion rather than defeat. His son **A** succeeds him as Fourth Raikage. **[M]**

##### The Ame Front (Everyone vs. Amegakure, and each other)
The Land of Rain is the war's grinder. Small, wet, strategically positioned between the great nations, and used as a battlefield by all of them. **Hanzō** maintains nominal control but the country is shattered. This is the crucible that produces **Akatsuki** in its original form.

---

#### Key Events of the Third War

##### Sakumo Hatake's Disgrace and Death
*Shortly before or early in the war.* **[CONFLICT — exact placement varies by reading]**

**Sakumo Hatake**, the "White Fang of the Konoha," a shinobi whose reputation reportedly exceeded the Sannin's, aborts a critical mission to save his squadmates. The mission's failure carries severe strategic consequences. He is publicly condemned — including by the very comrades he saved — and takes his own life. His son **Kakashi** internalizes the lesson as *the mission comes first*, which defines Kakashi's character until Obito breaks it.

##### The Kannabi Bridge Mission
*Approx. Year −20 to −19.* The single most consequential small-unit action in the setting.

Team Minato — **Kakashi Hatake** (newly promoted jōnin, age 13), **Obito Uchiha**, and **Rin Nohara** — is dispatched to destroy Kannabi Bridge and sever Iwa's supply line while Minato holds the front line.

**Sequence:**
1. Rin is captured by Iwa shinobi. Kakashi initially argues for continuing the mission; Obito refuses and goes after her alone. Kakashi follows.
2. In the rescue, Obito awakens his **Sharingan**.
3. A cave-in crushes Obito's right side. Believing he is dying, he has Rin transplant his left Sharingan into Kakashi's damaged eye socket as a promotion gift.
4. Kakashi and Rin escape and complete the mission. Obito is recorded KIA.
5. Obito survives, buried, and is recovered by **Madara Uchiha** — alive, ancient, and sustained by the Demonic Statue in a cavern beneath the mountains.

**This is the hinge of the entire setting.** See Book III §4 for what remains secret.

##### Rin Nohara's Death
*At or shortly after the war's end.* **[CONFLICT — placement varies]**

Kirigakure captures Rin and seals the **Three-Tails (Isobu)** inside her, intending to release the beast inside Konoha. Rin, aware of the plan and unwilling to be the weapon, steps into Kakashi's **Chidori** during a pursuit. Kakashi kills her by her own choice. Obito, arriving in time to witness only the killing blow, breaks completely — the moment that turns him toward the Eye of the Moon Plan.

**The Kiri operation was orchestrated by Madara and Obito.** No one in Konoha or Kiri knows this.

##### Minato's Ascension
Near the war's close, **Minato Namikaze** is named **Fourth Hokage**, chosen over Orochimaru — a slight Orochimaru does not forget. His appointment is widely credited with ending the Iwa front.

##### The War Ends
Konoha emerges nominally victorious but exhausted. A generation is missing. Peace treaties are signed but resolve nothing structural: Iwa's resentment, Kumo's ambition, Kiri's internal rot, and Suna's poverty all survive the ceasefire intact.

### PART THREE — ERA B: THE INTERBELLUM

**Approx. Year −14 to Year +12.** The long, poisoned peace.

---

#### Year 0 — The Nine-Tails Attack on Konoha

**Kushina Uzumaki**, jinchūriki of the Nine-Tails, gives birth to Naruto. Childbirth weakens a jinchūriki's seal — a known vulnerability, which is why the birth is conducted in secret outside the village under heavy guard.

A masked man (**Obito**, presenting himself as Madara) breaches the birth site, kills the guards and the midwife, takes the infant hostage to separate Minato from Kushina, extracts Kurama, and uses his Sharingan to place the beast under control. He unleashes it on Konoha.

**Outcome:**
- Minato frees Kurama from the genjutsu and drives Obito off via Flying Thunder God.
- Minato and Kushina seal Kurama's Yin half into the **Dead Demon Consuming Seal** (taking it with them into death) and the Yang half into the newborn **Naruto**, at the cost of both their lives.
- **Hiruzen Sarutobi** resumes the office of Hokage.
- The village is told a fraction of the truth. Naruto's parentage is declared an S-rank secret to protect him from his father's enemies. The Uchiha are quietly suspected of complicity because the attack bore Sharingan hallmarks — the beginning of the end for the clan.

##### Notable [CONFLICT]
Whether the Uchiha were formally accused or only informally suspected varies by source and by which character is describing events. Danzō's account, Itachi's account, and Tobirama's historical framing do not agree. Play it as *contested history*.

---

#### The Uchiha Question
*Years 0 to +7.*

Segregated to the village's edge and stripped of real influence, the Uchiha begin planning a coup. **Itachi Uchiha** is placed in ANBU as a double agent. **Shisui Uchiha** attempts to resolve the crisis with **Kotoamatsukami** but is intercepted by Danzō, who takes his right eye; Shisui gives Itachi his left eye and takes his own life.

**The Uchiha Massacre** *(Year +7)*: Itachi, working under orders from Danzō and the village elders and with Hiruzen's tortured acquiescence, eliminates the clan in a single night, sparing only **Sasuke**. He then defects, joins Akatsuki, and spends the rest of his life posing as a monster to give his brother a target and to keep Konoha's secrets buried.

---

#### Kirigakure — The Bloody Mist
*Roughly the same window.*

Under the **Fourth Mizukage Yagura** — secretly under Obito's genjutsu control **[M]** — Kirigakure earns its nickname:
- **Graduation by mutual killing:** Academy students are made to fight each other to the death for their headbands. **Zabuza Momochi** notoriously slaughtered an entire graduating class as an unenrolled child, which ended the practice. **[M]**
- **Bloodline purges:** Kekkei genkai users are hunted, driven underground, or lynched by their neighbors. This is how **Haku's** family died and how **Ice Release** was nearly eradicated.
- A civil war eventually deposes Yagura; **Mei Terumī** becomes Fifth Mizukage and works to rebuild the village's reputation.

---

#### Akatsuki's Transformation
*Approx. Year −10 to −5.* **[INF]**

Hanzō, fearing the growing influence of Yahiko's Akatsuki and manipulated by **Danzō**, sets a trap: Konan is taken hostage, and Nagato is forced to choose between her life and Yahiko's. **Yahiko impales himself on Nagato's kunai** to remove the choice.

Nagato awakens the full power of the **Rinnegan** in grief, annihilates Hanzō's forces, and — over the following years — kills Hanzō and everyone connected to him, seizing Amegakure entirely. Akatsuki is reborn as **Pain's** organization, guided from the shadows by Obito. It becomes a mercenary outfit funding itself while pursuing the tailed beasts.

---

#### Other Interbellum Threads

- **Orochimaru** is discovered conducting human experimentation, flees Konoha rather than face execution, briefly joins Akatsuki (paired with Sasori), then defects again and founds **Otogakure** in the Land of Rice Fields, approximately three years before Part I.
- **Sunagakure's decline** accelerates. Rasa seals **Shukaku** into his newborn son **Gaara**, sacrificing Gaara's mother Karura. The village later attempts to assassinate Gaara repeatedly.
- **The Hyūga Affair:** Kumogakure attempts to abduct the young heiress **Hinata** under cover of a peace treaty signing. Hiashi kills the abductor; Kumo demands his body in reparation; his twin brother **Hizashi** dies in his place, exploiting the Branch House caged bird seal. Kumo–Konoha relations remain toxic into the Fourth War.
- **The Twelve Guardian Ninja** protect the Fire Daimyō. **Asuma Sarutobi** serves and then leaves.
- **Kakashi** serves extensively in ANBU. **Yamato (Tenzō)**, a survivor of Orochimaru's Wood Release experiments, is recovered by Root and later reassigned.

### PART FOUR — ERA C: THE FOURTH GREAT NINJA WAR

**Approx. Year +16 to +17.** The war is short — a matter of days of open fighting — but total.

---

#### Causes

Following Akatsuki's capture of seven of the nine tailed beasts and Pain's assault on Konoha, the Five Kage convene a **Summit in the Land of Iron**, hosted by the samurai general **Mifune**. During the summit:
- **Obito**, posing as Madara Uchiha, appears and declares the **Eye of the Moon Plan**.
- He demands the Eight-Tails and Nine-Tails jinchūriki be handed over.
- When refused, he declares the **Fourth Great Ninja War**.
- **Sasuke Uchiha**, hunting Danzō, disrupts the summit and is declared an international criminal.

The five villages plus the Land of Iron form the **Allied Shinobi Forces** — the first unified command in shinobi history.

---

---

#### Phase Breakdown

##### Prelude — The Concealment of the Jinchūriki
Naruto and Killer B are hidden on the **Island Turtle** off Kumogakure. Naruto completes **Kurama's chakra** confrontation and **Sage Mode** training. Neither is told the war has begun — a decision that nearly costs the Allies the war when Naruto learns the truth and breaks out.

##### Day One — The Edo Tensei Offensive
- Reanimated shinobi are deployed against each division. The dead include former Kage, the Seven Ninja Swordsmen, Akatsuki members, and personal losses aimed at psychologically breaking specific Allied shinobi.
- **Key engagements:** the Fourth Division vs. the reanimated Kage; Kakashi's Third Division vs. Zabuza and the Swordsmen; Gaara vs. his father Rasa; Ōnoki vs. Mū.
- **The Allies learn that Edo Tensei cannot be killed** — only sealed, immobilized, or talked down.
- **Naruto and Killer B** arrive at the front. Naruto's **Kurama Chakra Mode** clones are distributed across the battlefield, transforming Allied morale and effectiveness.
- **Allied HQ is destroyed** by a Ten-Tails precursor attack. **Shikaku Nara** and **Inoichi Yamanaka** are killed, transmitting final orders as they die. Command devolves to **Shikamaru**.

##### Day Two — The Ten-Tails
- Obito and Madara complete the revival of the **Ten-Tails**, using the Gedō Statue and the collected tailed-beast chakra.
- The Allied Forces converge into a single mass engagement.
- **Orochimaru** returns, and **Sasuke** — having heard the truth of Itachi from the reanimated Hokage — reanimates **Hashirama, Tobirama, Hiruzen, and Minato** to learn the history of the village, then commits to protecting Konoha in his own way.
- **Obito becomes the Ten-Tails' jinchūriki.**
- **Naruto and Sasuke** fight alongside the Allied Forces; **Might Gai** opens the **Eighth Gate** against Madara.
- **Madara becomes the Ten-Tails' jinchūriki**, casts the **Infinite Tsukuyomi**, and traps virtually all of humanity in a dream.

##### Endgame — Kaguya
- **Black Zetsu** betrays Madara, revealing itself as the will of **Kaguya Ōtsutsuki**, and uses him as a vessel for her resurrection.
- **Team 7 (Naruto, Sasuke, Sakura, Kakashi)** plus Obito fight Kaguya across her linked dimensions and seal her.
- **Naruto and Sasuke** settle the Indra/Asura conflict at the **Valley of the End**. The Infinite Tsukuyomi is broken.

### PART FIVE — RUNNING THE ERAS

#### Which era for which campaign?

| If you want… | Run… |
|---|---|
| Small, lethal, personal tragedy | **Era A**, Iwa front, genin squad |
| Spy thriller, moral rot, assassination | **Era B**, ANBU or Root |
| Massed war, escalating cosmic horror | **Era C**, one squad inside a division |
| Village-building, politics, reconstruction | **Late Era B** or post-war |
| Wandering missing-nin / outlaw campaign | **Era B**, bingo book pressure |

#### Cross-era campaign structure **[GM]**
A strong long-form frame: start PCs as genin in **Era A**, timeskip them through **Era B** as jōnin and ANBU, and land them in **Era C** as division-level officers. Their Third War choices become Fourth War consequences. Their students become the next squad.

#### Pacing note
Both wars reward **compressed timelines**. The Fourth War's entire main action is roughly two days. Don't let a war arc sprawl over in-fiction months — the pressure comes from the clock.

# §2 — GEOGRAPHY & NATIONS

The physical world: lands, villages, terrain, borders, travel, and the specific places worth staging a scene in.

---
---

### PART ONE — THE FIVE GREAT NATIONS

Each great nation is a feudal state ruled by a **daimyō**, with a **hidden village** functioning as its military and intelligence arm. The daimyō holds the treasury; the Kage holds the force. This tension underlies most national politics — see Book I §3.

---

#### Land of Fire (Hi no Kuni)

**Village:** Konohagakure, the Village Hidden in the Leaves
**Symbol:** A stylized leaf
**Terrain:** Temperate deciduous and evergreen forest, river valleys, low mountains. Enormous trees — some of them Hashirama's Wood Release growth. Abundant water and arable land.
**Climate:** Four full seasons, warm humid summers, mild winters.
**Economy:** Agriculture, timber, and the largest shinobi mission economy in the world.

The wealthiest and most militarily dominant of the five nations in both eras. Its abundance is precisely what the other nations resent.

##### Konohagakure — Key Locations
- **Hokage Residence & Administration Building** — Attached to the Hokage Rock; houses the mission desk, archives, and Hokage's office.
- **Hokage Rock (Hokage Monument)** — Faces of every Hokage carved into the cliff. Minato's face is added late in Era A. A hollow chamber behind it is used for records and emergency command.
- **The Academy** — Central, adjacent to administration. Training grounds attached.
- **Training Grounds (numbered)** — Ground Three (the three posts and the memorial) is the traditional bell-test site. Ground 44, **the Forest of Death**, is a walled wilderness used for survival testing.
- **Memorial Stone (KIA Stone)** — Black stone inscribed with the names of shinobi killed in action. Kakashi's daily destination for years. **Obito's name is on it during Era A and B.**
- **Uchiha District** — Walled compound at the village's southeastern edge, deliberately distant from the center. Contains the Naka Shrine, beneath which lies the Uchiha stone tablet. **Abandoned and sealed after Year +7.**
- **Hyūga Compound** — Traditional, walled, strictly divided between Main and Branch houses.
- **Konoha Military Police Force HQ** — Uchiha-run in Era A. Dissolved after the massacre.
- **ANBU HQ / Torture and Interrogation Force** — Ibiki Morino's domain; the Yamanaka clan supplies mind-walkers.
- **Konoha Hospital** — Tsunade's institutional legacy; the model medical facility of the world.
- **Root's underground facility** — Beneath the village. Danzō's operation. Officially disbanded, actually not.
- **Ichiraku Ramen** — A stand. Matters more than its size suggests.
- **The Valley of the End** — On the border with the Land of Sound/Rice Fields. Colossal statues of Hashirama and Madara facing each other across a waterfall.

---

#### Land of Wind (Kaze no Kuni)

**Village:** Sunagakure, the Village Hidden in the Sand
**Symbol:** An hourglass / gourd shape
**Terrain:** Desert. Vast dune seas, rocky badlands, scattered oases, canyon systems. Sandstorms are a routine hazard and a tactical tool.
**Climate:** Blistering days, freezing nights.
**Economy:** Poor. Limited arable land, dependent on trade routes and mission income. **Chronically underfunded in both eras** — the Wind Daimyō's habit of contracting cheaper foreign shinobi (often Konoha's) is a standing humiliation.

##### Sunagakure — Key Locations
- **The village itself** is built inside a natural rock basin, entered through a narrow canyon cleft that functions as a chokepoint — a single defensible gate.
- **Kazekage Residence** — Domed central structure.
- **Puppet Workshops / Armories** — Suna's signature discipline. Chiyo's workshop is the most storied.
- **The Sealing Vault** — Where the puppets of past masters and dangerous artifacts are stored.
- **Desert oasis outposts** — Waystations along the trade routes; frequent mission objectives.

**Suna's relationship with Konoha:** Enemy in Era A. Uneasy ally, then betrayer (Chūnin Exams invasion), then genuine ally in Era C.

---

#### Land of Earth (Tsuchi no Kuni)

**Village:** Iwagakure, the Village Hidden in the Stones
**Symbol:** A stylized rock
**Terrain:** Sheer mountains, plateaus, canyons, and gorges. Very little flat ground. Extensive stone quarrying.
**Climate:** Cold, thin air at altitude.
**Economy:** Mining, stone, mineral wealth. Physically the hardest nation to invade.

##### Iwagakure — Key Locations
- The village is built into and around **rock spires and pillars**, connected by bridges and switchbacks. Vertical combat is the norm.
- **Tsuchikage's Office** — At the summit of the central spire.
- **The quarries and mines** — Both economic base and battlefield.
- **The Land of Earth's border with the Land of Grass** — The Third War's bloodiest ground. Kannabi Bridge is in this region.

**Iwa's grudge:** Iwagakure lost heavily to Minato in the Third War and never got over it. Ōnoki carries this personally into Era C, where he must serve alongside Konoha. Iwa shinobi in the Fourth War will have opinions about the Yellow Flash's son.

---

#### Land of Lightning (Kaminari no Kuni)

**Village:** Kumogakure, the Village Hidden in the Clouds
**Symbol:** A stylized cloud
**Terrain:** Extremely high mountains — the highest in the world — perpetually wreathed in cloud. Coastal on its eastern side; the **Island Turtle** and outlying islands belong to it.
**Climate:** Cold, storm-prone, thin air.
**Economy:** Strong. Militarily aggressive, with an active jinchūriki program.

##### Kumogakure — Key Locations
- The village sits **above the cloud layer**, on mountain peaks connected by bridges and cables. Approach is nearly impossible without being seen.
- **Raikage's Office** — Fortified; the Raikage's bodyguards (Darui, C, Shī) are always present.
- **The Unraikyō** — A perilous valley region.
- **The Island Turtle (Genbu)** — A colossal living turtle carrying an ecosystem on its shell, off the coast. Used as a training and concealment site; **Naruto and Killer B are hidden here at the start of Era C.**
- **The Falls of Truth** — On the Island Turtle. A waterfall where a shinobi confronts a manifestation of their own suppressed self. Passing it is a prerequisite for Killer B's jinchūriki training.

**Kumo's reputation:** Willing to abduct bloodline carriers, break treaties, and act unilaterally. The Hyūga Affair and the attempted abduction of Kushina Uzumaki are both Kumo operations.

---

#### Land of Water (Mizu no Kuni)

**Village:** Kirigakure, the Village Hidden in the Mist
**Symbol:** Four vertical wavy lines
**Terrain:** An archipelago. Rocky islands, dense perpetual fog, cold seas, sparse vegetation, lakes and inlets.
**Climate:** Cold, wet, low visibility year-round.
**Economy:** Isolated and insular. Poor international standing during the Bloody Mist period.

##### Kirigakure — Key Locations
- The village is built among **cylindrical rock towers and buildings**, permanently shrouded.
- **Mizukage's Office** — In the largest tower.
- **The Hōzuki Castle (Blood Prison)** — A maximum-security prison. **[MV]** for its most detailed depiction; treat carefully.
- **The Great Naruto Bridge** (Land of Waves, adjacent) — Named after Era B events; not relevant to Era A.

**Kiri's internal state:** In Era A/early B, Kirigakure is at its most dangerous and most self-destructive. Bloodline purges, lethal graduation exams, and a Mizukage under external mind control. In Era C, Mei Terumī has spent years rebuilding trust.

### PART TWO — MINOR COUNTRIES & VILLAGES

These are where the great nations actually fight. A great-nation war is fought almost entirely on someone else's land.

#### Land of Rain (Ame no Kuni)
**Village:** Amegakure. **Terrain:** Perpetual rainfall, industrial ruin, rivers, canals, rusting pipe-and-tower architecture.
Positioned between Fire, Earth, and Wind — which is why every great war is fought across it. Ruled by **Hanzō the Salamander** in Era A; seized by **Pain** in Era B. Under Pain, the rain itself is a surveillance system: Nagato senses every intruder through it. The most important minor country in the setting.

#### Land of Grass (Kusa no Kuni)
**Village:** Kusagakure. **Terrain:** Grasslands, rivers, gorges.
Sits between Fire and Earth. **The Kannabi Bridge is here.** Prime Era A campaign territory.

#### Land of Waterfalls (Taki no Kuni)
**Village:** Takigakure. **Terrain:** Cliffs, waterfalls, a hidden village behind the falls.
Small but wealthy and fiercely independent. Home of **Fū**, jinchūriki of the Seven-Tails, and of the **Hero's Water** — a substance that grants an enormous temporary power boost at the cost of the user's lifespan. **[A]** for most Hero's Water detail.

#### Land of Hot Water (Yu no Kuni)
**Village:** Yugakure — which abandoned the shinobi life to become a resort town. **Hidan** murdered his way out of it. Excellent for a "peace has consequences" storyline.

#### Land of Frost (Shimo no Kuni)
**Village:** Shimogakure. Cold, minor, borders Lightning and Earth.

#### Land of Whirlpools (Uzu no Kuni)
**Village:** Uzushiogakure — **destroyed during the Second War.** The Uzumaki homeland, renowned for fūinjutsu and longevity. Its ruins are a legitimate exploration site in any era. Konoha's uniform bears the Uzumaki spiral out of alliance and remembrance.

#### Land of Rice Fields → Land of Sound (Ta no Kuni / Oto no Kuni)
**Village:** Otogakure, founded by **Orochimaru approximately three years before Part I.** *Does not exist in Era A.* A network of hidden laboratories and bases rather than a true village.

#### Land of Iron (Tetsu no Kuni)
**No hidden village.** Ruled by **samurai** under General **Mifune**, who use chakra-flow-enhanced swordsmanship rather than ninjutsu. Snow-covered mountains, three great peaks. **Officially neutral** — which is why the Five Kage Summit is held here. In Era C, the samurai fight as part of the Allied Forces.

#### Land of Waves (Nami no Kuni)
Poor island nation with no shinobi village, dependent on sea trade. Notable only in Era B.

#### Other Named Lands
Land of Birds, Land of Tea, Land of Bears, Land of Vegetables, Land of Claws, Land of Keys, Land of Rivers, Land of Woods, Land of Fields, Land of Valleys, Land of Bean Jam, Land of Noodles, Land of Honey. Most are one-line references — **use them freely as mission destinations.** They exist precisely to be generic.

**[MV] / [A] lands — quarantine these unless you want them:** Land of Snow/Spring, Land of the Moon, Land of Demons, Land of the Sky, Land of the Sea.

#### Travel Speed **[GM — canon gives no consistent figures]**

Shinobi travel by rooftop, treetop, and chakra-assisted running. Use these as table baselines:

| Mode | Speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Civilian on foot | 3 mph / 24 mi per day | Merchants, escort clients |
| Ox cart / caravan | 2 mph / 16 mi per day | The reason escort missions are slow |
| Genin squad, sustained | 12 mph / 80 mi per day | Tree-hopping, rests included |
| Jōnin, sustained | 20 mph / 140 mi per day | Chakra-efficient movement |

| Body Flicker (Shunshin) | Tactical only | Not a travel method |
| Flying Thunder God | Instant | Requires a pre-placed marker |

**Rule of thumb for great-nation distances:** Konoha to the nearest great-nation border is roughly **3 days** at jōnin pace, **5–7 days** with a client in tow. Konoha to Suna is about **3 days** for a fast squad. **[GM]**

#### Terrain as a Tactical Resource

Nature transformation availability is terrain-dependent, and this should have mechanical weight at your table. This table implements the printed rule that costs *"may increase if techniques are overused or performed in extreme conditions"* **[SHEET]** — see **Book IV §1, Part Six**.

| Terrain | Favors | Penalizes | Suggested effect **[GM]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forest | Wood, Earth, Fire, Aburame, Inuzuka | Long-range, wide AoE | Cover everywhere; ranged attacks at disadvantage past 60 ft |
| Desert | Wind, Earth, Magnet Release | Water users without stored water | Water jutsu cost **+50% Chakra**; visibility drops in storms |
| Mountains | Earth, Lightning, Dust Release | Heavy armor, mounted movement | Difficult terrain; falling hazards |
| Open water / coast | Water, Kiri doctrine | Earth users | Water jutsu cost **−25% Chakra** |

| Rain (Amegakure) | Water, Lightning conduction | Fire | Fire jutsu deal half damage outdoors |
| Snow / Land of Iron | Ice, Water | Fire (fuel-poor) | Exhaustion checks without cold gear |
| Urban / village | Ambush, genjutsu, Kawarimi | Large AoE (collateral) | Substitution has abundant targets |

#### Weather & Environment Hazards
- **Sandstorm (Suna):** Heavily obscured, 1d6 damage per round unshielded, sensors impaired.
- **Lightning storm (Kumo):** Lightning jutsu +1 damage die; metal-carrying characters risk strikes.
- **Sea fog (Kiri):** The default state. Assume heavily obscured beyond 20 ft in any Kiri engagement.
- **Deep forest canopy (Fire):** Three-dimensional combat; falling damage is a real threat.

### PART FIVE — CAMPAIGN GEOGRAPHY BY ERA

#### Era A (Third War) — Where to set adventures
The action is on **the borders and in the minor countries**, not in the villages. Prioritize:
- **Land of Grass** — Iwa front, bridges, supply lines, forward camps.
- **Land of Rain** — Everyone's battlefield. Trench-warfare tone.
- **Land of Earth's border ranges** — Mountain warfare, cave systems.
- **Fire's northern border** — Kumo raids and counter-raids.

Villages appear as *between-mission* locations: resupply, debrief, funerals, promotions.

#### Era B (Interbellum) — Where to set adventures
Now the villages *are* the setting. Prioritize:
- **Inside Konoha** — Uchiha district tension, ANBU HQ, Root's tunnels, archives.
- **Kirigakure** — Purges, hidden bloodline families, the civil war.
- **Amegakure** — Under Pain's total surveillance. Nearly impossible to infiltrate.
- **Minor countries** — Missing-nin hideouts, bandit contracts, Otogakure's labs.

#### Era C (Fourth War) — Where to set adventures
The battlefield is a **single continuous front** across the Land of Lightning and the surrounding territory, with the Ten-Tails at the center. Prioritize:
- **Your division's sector** — Keep the PCs in one formation with one commander.
- **The Allied HQ** — Until it is destroyed.
- **The Island Turtle** — For anything involving Naruto and B before they reach the front.
- **Kaguya's dimensions** (endgame) — Lava, ice, desert, acid-sea, gravity-well. Each is a fully alien environment; use them for a surreal final act.

# §3 — POLITICS & POWER STRUCTURE

Who gives orders, who pays for them, and who can override whom. The material that turns a combat campaign into a political one.

---
---

### PART ONE — THE TWO-HEADED STATE

Every great nation runs on a permanent, unresolved tension:

**The daimyō holds the money. The Kage holds the force.**

| | Daimyō | Kage |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | Civil rule, taxation, treaties, formal war declaration | Military command, mission assignment, village internal law |
| **Selection** | Hereditary noble | Chosen for strength; approved by daimyō and village council |
| **Controls** | The national budget — including the village's funding | The only people capable of enforcing anything |
| **Weakness** | Cannot fight | Cannot pay for anything without approval |

The daimyō **funds and formally appoints** the Kage; the Kage **runs the village autonomously** in practice. A daimyō who displeases his village finds his contracts unfulfilled. A Kage who displeases his daimyō finds his budget cut.

**The Fire Daimyō** maintains the **Twelve Guardian Ninja** as a personal guard drawn from Konoha — a hedge against relying entirely on the Hokage.

**The Wind Daimyō** repeatedly contracted *Konoha* shinobi instead of his own Sunagakure, because they were cheaper. This is the single largest cause of Suna's decline and a direct contributor to Suna's willingness to betray Konoha later. It is an excellent campaign engine.

---
---

### PART TWO — THE KAGE

#### The Office

**Kage** (影, "Shadow") is the title of the leader of a great nation's hidden village. There are five. The title carries the implication of the strongest shinobi in the village, though in practice political acumen and clan backing matter as much as raw power.

**Regalia:** Ceremonial hat (kasa) with the village kanji, and a formal robe. Kage often ignore both.

**Succession:** By nomination — outgoing Kage, village council, or daimyō — with the village's advisory council ratifying. Succession by combat is not the norm, but a Kage who dies in office is replaced fast, and strength is always the tiebreaker.

#### The Hokage Line

| # | Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Hashirama Senju** | Founder. Wood Release. "God of Shinobi." |
| 2nd | **Tobirama Senju** | Created Edo Tensei, Flying Thunder God, the Academy, ANBU, and the Military Police. Died in the First War. |
| 3rd | **Hiruzen Sarutobi** | "The Professor." Longest-serving. Two separate terms. Dies during Era B. |
| 4th | **Minato Namikaze** | Appointed late in Era A. Dies at the Nine-Tails attack, Year 0. |
| 3rd (again) | **Hiruzen Sarutobi** | Resumes after Minato's death. |
| 5th | **Tsunade Senju** | Era B into Era C. Comatose for much of the Fourth War after shielding the Kage. |
| 6th | **Kakashi Hatake** | Post-Fourth War. |

#### The Kazekage Line
1st (unnamed) → 2nd → **3rd** (the strongest in Suna's history; wielder of the Iron Sand; **disappeared and was secretly killed and converted into a puppet by Sasori** — Suna searched for him for years) → **4th, Rasa** (sealed Shukaku into Gaara) → **5th, Gaara**.

**[CONFLICT]** The precise ordering of the Third Kazekage's disappearance relative to Sasori's defection is inferential. Both are placed roughly 20 years before Part I. Treat the sequence as: Sasori kills the Third → Sasori defects → the Third is declared missing → Rasa is elevated.

#### The Tsuchikage Line
1st **Ishikawa** → 2nd **Mū** ("Non-Person"; Dust Release; taught Ōnoki) → **3rd, Ōnoki** (in office through both eras; the longest-serving Kage of Era C) → 4th **Kurotsuchi** (post-war).

#### The Raikage Line
1st → 2nd → **3rd** (held off ten thousand shinobi alone for three days; died of exhaustion; possessed the strongest defense and the Hell Stab) → **4th, A** (his son; in office through Era C) → 5th **Darui** (post-war).

#### The Mizukage Line
1st → 2nd **Gengetsu Hōzuki** → **3rd** (in office during much of Era A) → **4th, Yagura Karatachi** (Three-Tails jinchūriki; the Bloody Mist era; **secretly under Obito's genjutsu control**) → **5th, Mei Terumī** → 6th **Chōjūrō** (post-war).

**[CONFLICT]** Whether the Third Mizukage was still in office at the time of the Kannabi Bridge mission, or Yagura had already ascended, is not firmly established. The Kiri operation to seal the Three-Tails into Rin implies an administration already compromised by Obito — which favors Yagura, but this is **[INF]**.

### PART THREE — KONOHA'S INTERNAL STRUCTURE

The most fully documented village. Use it as the template and vary it for the others.

#### The Hokage
Supreme authority within the village. Assigns missions, ranks shinobi, declares emergencies, and is the final word on internal law. In practice constrained by the council and by clan politics.

#### The Konoha Council (Advisors)
**Homura Mitokado** and **Koharu Utatane** — Hiruzen's former teammates. Formal advisors with real veto weight, particularly on matters of village security. Conservative, pragmatic, and willing to authorize atrocities framed as necessity. **They approved the Uchiha massacre.**

**Danzō Shimura** sits alongside them as a third power — head of **Root**, a rival to Hiruzen since childhood, and an advocate of a harder, more secretive village. He is not officially the Hokage's superior and behaves as though he is.

#### The Clan Heads' Council
The heads of Konoha's major clans convene on matters affecting the village as a whole. Not a formal legislature, but no Hokage governs against a united clan council. Members in Era A/B include the heads of the **Nara, Yamanaka, Akimichi, Aburame, Inuzuka, Hyūga, Sarutobi**, and — until Year +7 — **Uchiha**.

#### The Konoha Military Police Force
Internal law enforcement, **founded by Tobirama and staffed almost entirely by the Uchiha.** Their crest is the Uchiha fan.

The political reading matters: this was simultaneously a position of authority *and* a way to keep the Uchiha occupied with internal policing rather than external command, and under the eye of the village. The Uchiha understood this. It is the root of the coup.

**Dissolved after Year +7.**

#### ANBU (Ansatsu Senjutsu Tokushu Butai)
Assassination and Tactical Special Squad. Reports **directly to the Hokage**, outside the normal chain of command. Members wear animal masks, use codenames, and conceal their identities even from other shinobi. Recruitment is by invitation. Assignments include assassination, infiltration, bodyguard duty, and the retrieval or elimination of defectors.

Members are typically jōnin-level or exceptional chūnin. **Itachi joined at 11 and captained at 13.** Kakashi served extensively during Era B.

#### Root (Ne)
**Danzō's** organization. Nominally an ANBU subdivision; in practice a private army answering only to Danzō. Members are:
- Recruited as children, often orphans, and stripped of names and emotions.
- Marked with a **cursed seal on the tongue** that inflicts total paralysis if they attempt to speak of Root's affairs — a fūinjutsu gag that survives even mind-reading and, in some depictions, death. **[M]**

Officially disbanded by Hiruzen. Actually operational throughout Eras A and B.

**Root is the best campaign engine in the setting for a morally compromised game.** A Root PC cannot tell their squad who they work for — mechanically enforced.

#### The Torture and Interrogation Force
Headed by **Ibiki Morino**. The **Yamanaka clan** supplies mind-walking specialists (**Inoichi Yamanaka** is the premier practitioner). Handles captured enemies, suspected traitors, and sealed-memory retrieval.

#### The Konoha Barrier Team **[A]**
Maintains the village's detection barriers. Largely anime-elaborated; use if you want a "the barrier just went down" hook.

### PART FOUR — THE OTHER VILLAGES' STRUCTURES

#### Sunagakure
- **Kazekage** with a **Council of Elders** — **Chiyo** and **Ebizō** are the two most influential in Era A, effectively co-governing on matters of puppetry, poison, and sealing.
- **The Puppet Brigade** — Suna's signature combat formation.
- Notably **more willing than Konoha to use its own children as weapons** — the Gaara program is village policy, not one man's cruelty.

#### Iwagakure
- **Tsuchikage** with a heavily militarized command structure.
- Ōnoki's rule is long, personal, and grudge-driven. His son-in-law **Kitsuchi** and granddaughter **Kurotsuchi** are both senior officers — nepotism is normalized.
- Iwa fields **explosive-corps** and **stone-golem** doctrine; Deidara's Explosion Release originates from the Iwa lineage before his defection.

#### Kumogakure
- **Raikage** with two permanent bodyguards at all times (in Era C, **Darui** and **C**; earlier, **Mabui** serves as his aide and a specialist in the Heavenly Transfer teleportation technique).
- The most **centralized and autocratic** of the five. A gives an order and it is executed.
- Kumo maintains the setting's most successful **jinchūriki program**: it holds two beasts (Eight and Two Tails) and, uniquely, achieved a *cooperative* relationship between host and beast with Killer B.

#### Kirigakure
- **Mizukage** with a small advisory circle; **Ao** and **Chōjūrō** are Mei's key aides in Era C.
- The **Seven Ninja Swordsmen of the Mist** are a formal institution — an elite unit defined by the inheritance of seven named blades. See Book III §3.
- During the Bloody Mist, real authority sat with whoever controlled Yagura — that is, Obito.

#### Amegakure
- **Hanzō** (Era A): absolute personal rule. Hanzō's power is his own; the village is an extension of him.
- **Pain** (Era B): total surveillance theocracy. Nagato is worshipped as a god. **The rain is a sensor net** — Nagato perceives anyone within the country who is not shielded. **Konan** administers day-to-day.

#### The Land of Iron
- **Samurai**, not shinobi. Sword-based, chakra-flow combat. **General Mifune** commands.
- Formally neutral, which makes it the venue for the Five Kage Summit.
- Their neutrality is an *institutional* stance, not pacifism — they fight in Era C.

### PART FIVE — DIPLOMACY, TREATIES & LAW

#### Between Villages
- **Treaties** are signed between daimyō, not Kage, but enforced (or ignored) by villages.
- **Peace does not mean trust.** All five villages run espionage against each other continuously in all three eras.
- **The Hyūga Affair** is the canonical example of a treaty used as cover for an operation: Kumo signed a peace agreement and attempted an abduction the same night.

#### The Kage Summit
An extraordinary convocation of all five Kage. Historically rare. Two matter:
1. **The founding-era summit** that distributed the tailed beasts.
2. **The Land of Iron Summit** (Era C) that produced the Allied Shinobi Forces.

Protocol: each Kage attends with **two bodyguards**, seated by nation, hosted by a neutral party. Weapons permitted. Fights break out.

#### Missing-nin and the Bingo Book
A shinobi who abandons their village is a **nuke-nin** (missing-nin). Consequences:
- Entry in the **Bingo Book** — an international register of targets with bounties, threat ratings, and known techniques.
- **Hunter-nin** are dispatched to kill and dispose of the body — specifically to prevent the corpse's secrets (bloodlines, seals, techniques) from falling into foreign hands. Kirigakure's hunter corps are the most feared.
- Defection is the highest crime. Knowledge is the asset being protected, not the person.

#### Village Law
Village law is whatever the Kage says it is, moderated by council and clan. Notable standing offenses:
- Revealing village secrets (capital)
- Desertion (capital)
- Unauthorized use of forbidden techniques (varies; Orochimaru's experiments qualified)
- Clan-internal law is largely **self-administered** — the Hyūga branch seal is legal because the Hyūga say it is.

The first unified command in shinobi history, and structurally fragile because of it.

#### Divisional Assignment
Critically, divisions are organized **by combat range and specialty, not by village.** Shinobi from five formerly hostile nations are deliberately mixed into the same squads.

| Division | Specialty | Commander |
|---|---|---|
| First | Mid-range combat | Darui (Kumo) |
| Second | Short-to-mid-range | Kitsuchi (Iwa) |
| Third | Short-range combat | Kakashi Hatake (Konoha) |
| Fourth | Long-range combat | Gaara (Suna) |

| Sensor | Detection and coordination | Ao (Kiri) |

#### Communication
**Inoichi Yamanaka** operates a mind-link network from HQ, relaying orders instantly across the entire front. **Ino** serves as a relay node in the field. This is the Allies' single greatest tactical advantage — and when HQ is destroyed and Inoichi and Shikaku are killed, the network's collapse is a genuine crisis. **Shikamaru** inherits command.

#### Structural Weaknesses — use these
1. **Trust.** Iwa shinobi taking orders from Konoha's Kakashi. Kumo shinobi fighting beside Hyūga. Play the friction.
2. **The White Zetsu problem.** Zetsu clones perfectly impersonate individual Allied shinobi, including memories and mannerisms. Detection eventually relies on sensors, Byakugan, and Naruto's negative-emotion sensing. **Before that, nobody can trust anyone.**
3. **The Edo Tensei problem.** The enemy fields your own honored dead — teachers, parents, former Kage — who cannot be killed and who know your techniques.
4. **Command decapitation.** HQ is a single point of failure and the enemy knows it.

# §4 — THE SHINOBI SYSTEM

How a person becomes a shinobi, what rank means, how missions are assigned and paid, and the daily texture of the profession. This is the file to keep open during ordinary play.

---
---

### PART ONE — THE ACADEMY

#### Purpose
Founded by **Tobirama Senju**, the Second Hokage. Before the Academy, shinobi training was clan-internal and secret. The Academy's real innovation was **producing loyal village shinobi rather than loyal clan shinobi** — a political act disguised as an educational one.

#### Enrollment
- Typical entry age: **6–8.** Graduation: **12** in peacetime.
- Open to any citizen; clan children attend alongside civilians, though clan children arrive pre-trained and it shows.
- **In wartime, graduation age collapses.** Era A routinely graduates children at 5–9. Kakashi graduated at 5, made chūnin at 6, and jōnin at 12–13. Itachi graduated at 7. These are extreme cases but not unique — a war needs bodies.

#### Curriculum
| Subject | Content |
|---|---|
| **Chakra fundamentals** | Molding, control exercises (leaf-sticking, tree-walking is post-graduation) |
| **The Academy Three** | Clone, Transformation, Body Replacement — see Jutsu Compendium, E-Rank |
| **Shurikenjutsu** | Kunai and shuriken accuracy from static and moving positions |
| **Taijutsu** | Basic sparring forms |
| **History & ethics** | Village founding, the Will of Fire (Konoha), the shinobi code |
| **Tactics** | Squad formations, trap theory, basic reconnaissance |
| **Survival** | Field medicine, foraging, escape (Rope Escape Technique) |

#### Graduation
Passing the Academy exam grants the **forehead protector (hitai-ate)** and the rank of **genin**.

**Variant practices worth noting:**
- **Konoha:** Written exam plus a practical test, historically the Clone Technique.
- **Kirigakure, Bloody Mist era:** Students were made to **fight each other to the death** in pairs, and only survivors graduated. Ended after **Zabuza Momochi**, not even an enrolled student, killed an entire graduating class. **[M]**
- **Era A generally:** Standards are waived. Field promotion is common.

#### Team Assignment
Graduates are assigned in **three-genin cells under one jōnin sensei.** Composition is deliberate — teams are balanced for capability and sometimes for political reasons (the Ino-Shika-Chō formation is reconstituted every generation by tradition).

**A jōnin sensei may fail their assigned team outright.** Kakashi failed every team he was given before Team 7 — the bell test is a real filter, not a formality.

---

### PART TWO — RANKS

#### Academy Student (Gakusei)
Not yet a shinobi. No mission authority, no combat deployment (except in wartime emergency).
**Level 0–1.**

#### Genin (下忍) — "Low Ninja"
Entry rank. Always deployed as part of a three-person cell with a jōnin leader. Handles D-rank and, with supervision, C-rank work.

Genin status is not necessarily temporary — plenty of shinobi remain genin their entire careers, either by choice, lack of opportunity, or repeated exam failure. **Might Duy**, the "Eternal Genin," is the definitive example: a lifelong genin who could open all eight gates.
**Level 1–4.**

#### Chūnin (中忍) — "Middle Ninja"
The first rank of **command**. A chūnin is qualified to lead a squad, make tactical decisions independently, and take responsibility for subordinates' lives. Promotion is judged on **leadership and judgment**, not raw power — which is why a candidate can lose their Chūnin Exam match and still be promoted.

Chūnin may serve as Academy instructors, mission desk staff, or field squad leaders.
**Level 5–8.**

#### Tokubetsu Jōnin (特別上忍) — "Special Jōnin"
Jōnin-level mastery in **one specific discipline**, without the breadth required for full jōnin rank. Examples: interrogation, poison, sensory, barrier work, weapons.
Not a lesser rank so much as a *narrower* one. Anko Mitarashi, Ibiki Morino, and Genma Shiranui are tokubetsu jōnin.
**Level 7–10.**

#### Jōnin (上忍) — "High Ninja"
Elite. Broad mastery, typically multiple chakra natures, and full command authority. Jōnin lead genin cells, run A-rank missions independently, and form the backbone of any war effort.
**Level 9–14.**

#### ANBU Operative
Not a rank in the ladder — a **posting**. Drawn from jōnin and exceptional chūnin. Reports directly to the Kage. Masked, codenamed, deniable.
**Level 11–15.**

#### Kage (影)
One per village. See Book I §3.
**Level 18–20.**

#### Unofficial Designations
- **Sannin** — An honorific, not a rank. Bestowed by Hanzō on Jiraiya, Tsunade, and Orochimaru. Denotes legendary individual capability.
- **Missing-nin (nuke-nin)** — A defector. Ranked in the Bingo Book by threat, typically **A** or **S**.
- **Hunter-nin** — A specialist posting focused on eliminating and disposing of defectors.
- **Sage (Sennin)** — One who has mastered senjutsu. Outside the rank system entirely.

### PART THREE — THE CHŪNIN EXAMS

Held biannually, hosted in rotation among allied villages. In Era A the exams are **suspended or heavily curtailed** — the war provides its own promotion mechanism, namely dead superiors.

#### The Real Purpose
Publicly: a fair test of readiness. Actually: **a controlled demonstration of military strength** in front of visiting daimyō and clients. Villages showcase their genin to attract mission contracts. A strong showing is worth more than the promotions.

**This is the single best political set-piece in the setting.** Every village in the arena is scouting, and at least one is lying about something.

#### Standard Three-Stage Structure
1. **Written / Information-Gathering Stage** — Ostensibly a written test; actually a test of whether you can cheat without being caught, and of whether you'll accept an unacceptable risk. Ibiki Morino's tenth-question gambit is the classic form.
2. **Survival Stage** — A wilderness or hazard environment with a competitive objective (the Forest of Death's scroll retrieval). Tests endurance, teamwork, and willingness to fight peers.
3. **Tournament Stage** — Public one-on-one matches before the Kage, daimyō, and clients. **Winning is not the criterion.** Judges evaluate leadership, decision-making, and grace under pressure.

#### Promotion Criteria
A candidate is promoted if the judges believe they can **lead a squad and bring them home.** Shikamaru Nara forfeits his match and is the only promotion from his exam.

### PART FOUR — MISSIONS

#### Mission Ranks

| Rank | Danger | Assigned to | Typical content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D** | Negligible | Genin | Chores, labor, pet retrieval, weeding, babysitting. Village-internal. |
| **C** | Low | Genin (led) / Chūnin | Escort duty, courier, minor bandit trouble, low-risk travel |
| **B** | Moderate | Chūnin / Jōnin | Espionage, guarding against enemy shinobi, retrieval, protection |
| **A** | High | Jōnin | Assassination, sabotage, high-value escort, war-front operations |
| **S** | Extreme | Elite jōnin / Kage-tier | State-level assassination, invasion, forbidden-technique recovery |

**The critical rule:** *a mission's assigned rank reflects the client's briefing, not the reality.* Tazuna's escort was contracted as a C-rank and was in fact an A-rank because the client lied about who was hunting him. **Under-ranked missions are the most reliable plot generator in the setting.**

#### Mission Pay **[DB]**

| Rank | Payment range (ryō) |
|---|---|
| **D** | 5,000 – 50,000 |
| **C** | 30,000 – 100,000 |
| **B** | 150,000 – 200,000 |
| **A** | 150,000 – 1,000,000 |
| **S** | 1,000,000 and up |

**[CONFLICT]** The ryō's real-world value is widely cited as roughly 10 ryō ≈ ¥10, but this conversion is not authoritatively sourced. Use ryō as a self-contained currency and don't convert.

**Who gets paid:** The **village** is the contracting party. The shinobi receive a portion; the village takes the rest for operations, the Academy, the hospital, and the treasury. A missing-nin taking private contracts keeps everything — one of the few genuine advantages of defection.

#### ⚠ THE SQUAD SHARE **[GM — REPAIRED. "A portion" was never a number, and the economy broke without one.]**

> **The bug.** The table above is the **contract value paid to the village.** The rules said squads receive *"a portion"* and never said how much — so at the table the whole contract landed in the players' pockets. **A single D-rank mission at 5,000–50,000 ryō buys between twelve and a hundred and twenty-five flash bombs**, against Book II's escape kit at 250–1,500 a piece. The escape kit is explicitly designed to be *"consumables… what turns D-rank mission pay into a meaningful reward."* **It was not a meaningful anything. It was pocket change from mission one.**

| Rank | Squad's share of contract value | Then split |
|---|---:|---|
| **Genin** | **10%** | Evenly among the squad; **sensei takes no cut** |
| **Chūnin** | **15%** | Evenly |
| **Jōnin** | **25%** | Evenly |
| **S-Rank / independent under village seal** | **40%** | Evenly |
| **Missing-nin / private contract** | **100%** | And no hospital, no requisition, no backup |

**Worked example — the intended feel.** A Genin squad of three clears a D-rank at 15,000 ryō. The squad share is **1,500**, or **500 each.** That is one flash bomb and change, or half a smoke bomb short of two. **Now the escape kit is a decision.**

**Worked example — the top end.** A jōnin pair clears an A-rank at 600,000. Their share is **150,000**, or **75,000 each** — which buys a chakra-shroud cloak, a storage scroll and a season of not thinking about money. *That is correct. Jōnin are supposed to be comfortable.*

> **Hazard pay, salvage and claims sit outside this table** and are the GM's to price. They are also how a long-running independent unit becomes genuinely wealthy without ever taking an S-rank — which is exactly how **遊撃班 Yūgekihan** ended up not needing to track money at all.

#### Standing costs the village covers, and stops covering

| | Genin / Chūnin | Jōnin+ | Missing-nin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard kit, replacement gear | **Free** | Free | **Buy it** |
| Hospital, medical-nin | **Free** | Free | **Nothing** |
| Specialist gear, sealed scrolls | Requisition, approval needed | Requisition | **Black market, at 3× list** |
| Housing | Subsidised | Own it | — |

**This is why defection is expensive** and why the pay difference is not the windfall it looks like on paper.

#### The Mission Desk
Located in the administration building, staffed by chūnin and overseen (in Konoha) by the Hokage in person for anything above C-rank. Squads receive briefings, scroll assignments, and — critically — **the client's stated version of events.**

#### Mission Structure at the Table **[GM]**
A reliable four-beat shape:
1. **The briefing** — what the client says.
2. **The journey** — travel, terrain, first contact, the first hint the briefing is wrong.
3. **The reversal** — the actual threat, at a rank above what was assigned.
4. **The cost** — the squad succeeds, and something is spent: a technique, a relationship, a life, a principle.

Beat 4 is what makes it *Naruto* rather than generic fantasy. Full mission generator in Book IV §5.

### PART FIVE — EQUIPMENT & UNIFORM

#### The Forehead Protector (Hitai-ate)
A metal plate bearing the village symbol, mounted on cloth. Worn on the forehead, but positioning is personal — as an eyepatch (Kakashi), around the neck (Hinata), on the arm, on the belt.

**A scratched-through symbol marks a missing-nin.** The gouge is deliberate and permanent, a formal renunciation. Zabuza, Itachi, Kisame, Deidara, and Sasori all wear them. It is the most legible social signal in the setting: everyone who sees it knows exactly what you are.

### PART SIX — CULTURE, PROTOCOL & DAILY LIFE

#### The Shinobi Code
The formal doctrine every Academy student learns:
> *A shinobi is a tool. Emotion compromises the mission. The mission comes before the individual. A shinobi does not weep.*

**This is the setting's central thesis and its central argument.** Sakumo Hatake broke it to save his comrades and was destroyed for it. Kakashi enforced it until Obito told him that *those who break the rules are trash, but those who abandon their comrades are worse than trash.* Every generation relitigates it.

**The Will of Fire** is Konoha's counter-doctrine: the village is a family, the previous generation exists to protect the next, and loyalty is to people rather than to orders. Hiruzen preaches it. Danzō considers it a fatal weakness.

#### Squad Culture
- The **three-person cell** is the fundamental unit in every village. Formations are drilled: Manji formation (defensive triangle), pursuit formations, and clan-specific arrangements like Ino-Shika-Chō.
- **Teammate bonds are institutionally encouraged and personally ruinous.** This is the point.

#### Funerary Practice
- Konoha's **Memorial Stone** lists the KIA. The dead are honored publicly; ANBU and black-ops deaths are not.
- **Hunter-nin dispose of missing-nin bodies entirely** — burning or dissolving the corpse so that bloodlines and seals cannot be harvested. A defector gets no grave.

#### Money & Ordinary Life
Shinobi are salaried professionals with mission bonuses. Villages contain ordinary civilians — shopkeepers, farmers, teachers, restaurateurs — who vastly outnumber shinobi and who mostly want nothing to do with them. Konoha's civilian population lives normally, protected by and slightly afraid of its own military.

#### Honorifics & Address **[Useful for dialogue]**
- **-sensei** — teacher, and specifically a jōnin squad leader. Used long after graduation.
- **-sama** — high respect. Used for Kage ("Hokage-sama"), clan heads, daimyō.
- **-san** — standard polite.
- **-kun / -chan** — familiar; -kun for juniors and young men, -chan for children and intimates.
- **senpai / kōhai** — senior and junior within an institution.
- **danna** — "boss"/"master," used with a particular sardonic edge (Deidara to Sasori).
- **Rank as address:** "Taichō" (Captain) in ANBU; "Shishō" (master) for a formal teacher, as Sakura uses for Tsunade.

Family names precede given names in formal address. Uchiha Itachi, Hatake Kakashi.

### PART SEVEN — RANK PROGRESSION AT THE TABLE **[GM]**

Tie shinobi rank to character level so promotion is a real event.

| Level | Rank achieved | Suggested trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Genin | Character creation / Academy graduation |
| 5 | Chūnin | Chūnin Exams, or field promotion after leading a squad through a crisis |
| 9 | Jōnin | Recommendation by a jōnin + demonstrated independent command |
| 11 | ANBU eligibility | Invitation only; Kage's discretion |
| 15 | Elite jōnin / Kage candidate | Reputation; a named technique of their own |
| 18 | Kage | Nomination, succession crisis, or war |

**Field promotion rule for Era A:** In wartime, a PC may be promoted one rank *immediately* if they take command after their squad leader dies and bring survivors home. This happened constantly and it should happen at your table.

#### Rank and Mission Authority
- A genin **may not** accept a mission without a supervising chūnin or jōnin.
- A chūnin **may** lead a squad and accept up to B-rank.
- Accepting a mission above your authorized rank is an offense — but "we didn't know it was A-rank until we got there" is the standard, universally-understood excuse, and it is usually accepted.
